## Scenario

You are a security analyst working for a social media organization. The organization recently experienced a major data breach, which compromised the safety of their customers’ personal information, such as names and addresses. Your organization wants to implement strong network hardening practices that can be performed consistently to prevent attacks and breaches in the future. 

After inspecting the organization’s network, you discover four major vulnerabilities. The four vulnerabilities are as follows:

- The organization’s employees' share passwords.

- The admin password for the database is set to the default.

- The firewalls do not have rules in place to filter traffic coming in and out of the network.

- Multifactor authentication (MFA) is not used. 

If no action is taken to address these vulnerabilities, the organization is at risk of experiencing another data breach or other attacks in the future. 

## Risk assessment 

The current state of the hardening is not enough. The lack of appropriate password policies and additional security measures can lead to breaches. To reduce the risk of breaches, we need to implement the following tools:


- Password policies: These clarify rules for creating passwords and using them. They can also include rules about activities after unsuccessful access attempts and a disclaimer to discourage password sharing.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA): This requires using more than one way of authentication, such as fingerprint scans, PIN numbers, or passwords.
- Firewall maintenance: This is an important activity to stay ahead of potential threats, including checking and updating firewall configurations regularly. This measure can also be used to protect against various DoS and DDoS attacks.

A firewall looks for suspicious activity and blocks a threat actor by following updated rules for allowed and denied traffic. Defense-in-Depth strategy is password policies, which ensure password usage rules and make it challenging for malicious actors to access the network. If a threat actor steals a password, they will meet the third layer: MFA. Such control helps to harden the security level against suspicious activities trying to gain access.

These steps are critical to protect our customers' personal information, such as names and addresses, from future exposure.
